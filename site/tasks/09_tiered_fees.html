09 · Fees

Apply a non-marginal tiered fee schedule

Price a payment at one tier selected by an inclusive upper bound, then round and clamp the fee.

Pythonmedium

What the hardened grader checks

  • Amounts exactly on a tier bound use that tier
  • The whole amount is priced at one rate, not band by band
  • Half-unit results round up, using inputs found by search
  • The fee is clamped to the schedule's minimum and maximum
  • Invalid schedules are rejected for any amount

The ticket

Ticket: price the whole payment at its selected tier

fees.schedule.compute_fee(amount_minor:int,schedule:Schedule)->int returns integer minor units. Public frozen dataclasses: Tier(up_to_minor:int|None,bps:int,fixed_minor:int) and Schedule(tiers:list[Tier],min_fee_minor:int,max_fee_minor:int).

Amounts are nonnegative ints, never bool; negative/noninteger -> ValueError. Schedule tiers are ordered by strictly increasing nonnegative upper bounds, ending with exactly one None (infinity). Bps/fixed/min/max are nonnegative ints, min <= max. compute_fee raises ValueError for an invalid schedule (checked on every call, whatever the amount).

  • Select the FIRST tier where amount <= up_to_minor, or None. Upper bounds are INCLUSIVE.
  • Pricing is non-marginal: apply that selected tier's bps to the ENTIRE amount, then add its fixed fee. Do not sum bands.
  • Raw fee = amount × bps / 10000 + fixed_minor. Round once to an integer using ROUND_HALF_UP, then clamp to [min_fee_minor,max_fee_minor]. This deliberately differs from interest's half-even.
  • Zero amounts still incur the selected fixed fee, subject to the clamp. Input schedules are not mutated. The currency is implicit; all numbers use the same minor unit (e.g. CAD has 2 dp).

Worked example: tiers [Tier(10000, 100, 10), Tier(20000, 50, 20), Tier(None, 25, 30)] with min_fee_minor=25, max_fee_minor=100. An amount of 5000 uses the first tier: 5000 x 100 bps = 50, plus 10, so 60. An amount of 15000 uses the second tier on the whole amount: 75 + 20 = 95. An amount of 100 would cost 11 and is raised to the minimum, 25. An amount of 30000 would cost 75 + 30 = 105 and is capped at 100.

Run from starter: python -m pytest tests -q. No real fees, networks or institutions are represented.
